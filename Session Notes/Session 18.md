# Session 18 — *After the Mountain Spoke*

**Locales:** Mountain cave and mine approach · Guard camp overlook · Road back to Hýrda · Ysidor’s house
**Time:** Night → deep night, around 2–3 a.m. → long rest

---

## 1) After Hýr Spoke — Static, Silence, and the Stunned

Hýr had reached the party in the cave: aware of their plans, weakened and half-asleep, but still offering what little help it could. The voice seemed familiar with more than one of them. It spoke of hearing the prayers of followers even in its diminished state, and of once moving its body.

But the oldest names and words did not come cleanly. They were swallowed by static. Nyx, on a nat 20, had understood the shape of it: some deep magic was preventing certain words and names from being said.

Ellara had been keeping watch outside while the others finished setting explosives within. The stone goliaths had heard Hýr too. Many stood frozen where they were, mouths open, stunned by the fact that their god, goddess, higher power had spoken and then gone back to sleep.

Nyx, noticing the sending stones and remembering they had given one to Meri, turned to Witty.

**Nyx:** "Can you make one of these?"

**Witty:** "I can try."

Nyx pressed the point: they were missing one now, and if Witty could make one, he should make two and keep one. Witty could, in fact, make sending stones through his artificer replicate magic item plans, but only after a long rest with tinkerer’s tools in hand. The answer would have to wait.

**Witty:** "Yeah, I can do that for us, but I'm pretty tired. So maybe later, after a long rest."

---

## 2) Keepers of Stories — Ellara Reaches for History

Ellara was still trying to process what had happened. Through the open sending-stone channel to Ysidor and Meri, she asked if the village had books, an old record keeper, or anyone who could explain the local history of Hýr.

**Ellara:** "Is there any books or something about the history of the earth?"

**Ellara:** "I need to confirm some things."

**Ellara:** "I just can't process what just happened."

Meri coughed.

**Ysidor:** "That's her."

**Ysidor:** "That's what I'm training for."

Ellara had thought of Ysidor as a defender, not as someone being trained to keep the story. Meri cut in with dry affection.

**Meri:** "Little Hill, you're really not great on explanations, are you?"

**Meri:** "We need to talk about that at some point. You in detail is not great."

Then she clarified: Ysidor was her chosen successor.

Nyx wondered whether he might have anything useful in his own library. On reflection (**Nyx History Nat 20**), he knew he had historical books and, more importantly, meticulous diaries from the years since everything changed. He had never written much about actual jobs, but he had kept notes detailed enough to cross-reference what he saw, heard, and survived.

Those diaries were back at his place. Here, in the mountain, he could only remember that they existed.

Berridin, meanwhile, was running out of strength.

**Berridin:** "I'm just so tired."

Witty looked overwhelmed in a different way. To him, the divine had always seemed near, his family’s blessing a reward for hard work and dedication. Hearing Hýr did not break that belief. It confirmed it, leaving him wide-eyed and awash in awe.

**Witty:** "Spiritual moment."

He shared the feeling with Berridin as best he could, smiling and patting him on the head. Around them, Meri moved among her people one by one, steadying them after what they had heard. She seemed less shaken than most, but even she had to gather herself.

---

## 3) The Charges — Wire, Buttons, and Backup Plans

Witty helped finish the explosive work (**Witty Intelligence with advantage 11**). The earlier placements were settled, though he noticed some miners adjusting parts of the setup slightly from what he intended. Not badly, just differently. Everyone was still making good time.

Ellara kept watch with the owl while the party discussed how the cave would actually be detonated when the army arrived in about a week.

**Witty:** "Am I going to have a comically long fuse on a reel that I'm having to wheel outside?"

Berridin wondered if a sending stone could trigger a fuse, but the stones did not seem to create sparks or work through electricity. So he proposed something more personal: run in disguised as Meri, cast invisibility, sneak out, and use a cantrip on the way out if needed. Mostly, he wanted a backup plan.

**Berridin:** "I am not gonna get stuck in this trouble."

**Berridin:** "I'm not gonna die."

While they worked, Ellara tried to reconcile the revelation with scripture (**Ellara Religion 13**). Most of what Hýr described did not directly contradict the Eternal Light. Witty recalled the teaching that Nites moved the mountains and created the path that exists today. The point of conflict was sharper than that: Hýr had said someone unknown asked, and Hýr moved itself. Scripture said Nites was the one who did it.

Ellara kept watch and made notes for herself. She would need to ask Proctor Eduard later.

Once the stone goliaths had calmed, Meri indicated that the preparations looked ready. Witty set the detonation system with care: the main trigger inside the cave, and a backup trigger outside, around the corner, hidden in a fern. He walked with a large reel of wire, concealing the line behind twigs and leaves.

The main trigger was adjusted so Berridin could reach it if needed. By the rules, a high jump is 3 + Strength modifier feet, with reach adding half the jumper’s height above themself. Berridin, at about three feet tall, could jump and reach high enough to hit something around five or six feet up.

**Witty:** "We'll put the big red button six feet up."

The hidden backup trigger outside was not a big red button. It was a giant plunger hidden in the ferns. No roll was needed; it was simple technology.

---

## 4) The Owl’s Watch — A Clear Way Out

Before the group left the cave, they checked whether they needed to sneak everyone out.

**Ellara:** "I haven't seen any of the guards."

Through the owl, Ellara fully rode its senses for a moment (**Ellara Perception Nat 20**). At first it was dizzying, then she adapted. The owl became almost a middle processor between her own mind and the night.

From above, she saw one guard in the watchtower, not paying attention, sharpening a blade. The guard campsite was visible, its fires crackling. She smelled roasting meat and felt the owl’s hunger for it. Beyond them, the mountain range stretched dark and vast.

The coast was clear.

**Ellara:** "Yeah, it looks like it's clear. I think you guys are good to go."

**Ellara:** "Don't be afraid. You don't have to worry too much right now. They seem to be otherwise occupied."

She watched the group come out through the owl’s eyes. Only once she knew they were safe did she pull back into herself. The world felt muted afterward, quieter and smaller than it had through the owl.

**Ellara:** "You're interesting."

**Ellara:** "Now I've tasted the freedom of soaring..."

Before breaking contact, she sent the owl gratitude and the hope that they could speak again. The owl answered with a hunting screech in the night, then dove after prey. Ellara left before seeing the end of the hunt.

She rejoined the group quieter than usual, shaken by the voice of something tied to one of the most important stories in her faith. She wanted answers, but she waited until they were back at the village.

---

## 5) On the Road — The Stories Hýrda Keeps

Meri led the miners and stone goliaths back toward Hýrda. The rescued group moved in hushed tones, nearly whispering.

Ellara approached Meri on the road.

**Ellara:** "Maybe tomorrow, but I would love to hear the story of how Hýr came to be in this location... or what do you know about the origins of Hýr? I just don't understand."

Meri admitted that this was not normally how she experienced Hýr. There were stories, though, and she could tell them.

**Meri:** "There are no real secrets among the village and such. My job is to keep the stories alive. It's not to hide them."

Unlike the Eternal Light, Meri said, her role was not to hide stories. Perhaps it would even be a good moment for Ysidor to remember some of the stories he was supposed to have memorized by now.

Meri had things to think on too. She did not always hear her god so strongly.

**Meri:** "I can tell you what we know and what has been passed among myself and my lineage, but also from the other stone goliaths that we have met and encountered over the years, and their stories that we share."

Ellara bowed a little, admitting she felt far from the elders at the seminary and did not know where else to get answers.

**Meri:** "I am sure they could help you and tell you all what they have recorded in history. And maybe our stories will align, and maybe they are different."

**Meri:** "I can only tell you the stories and memories and songs of our people."

**Meri:** "Whether you take them for truth or not, that is to you."

Ysidor was present, though distracted by a bird, and Meri gave him the look of a teacher remembering unfinished homework.

**Ysidor:** "Story? What story, exactly?"

**Meri:** "The story of Hýr. You know, part of the homework you should be doing as my heir?"

**Ysidor:** "Definitely the homework. Definitely on the list of things to do."

Then he claimed Berridin was calling him and hurried ahead.

**Meri:** "Not the most studious one."

**Ellara:** "His heart is in the right place."

**Meri:** "Exactly. There are a few that I could say that about. And that is what matters to Hýr. It's not so much what the mind knows, but what the heart sees."

Ellara said she needed to write down what had happened. It might change a lot of things. Something was moving.

**Meri:** "That I agree. There are movements beyond us."

Meri walked off, and Ellara was so deep in thought she barely noticed.

---

## 6) Little Hill — Succession, Trust, and Signs

By the time they reached the village, it was the middle of the night, around 2 or 3 a.m. Nyx settled outside Ysidor’s house in trance, propped against the wall, taking time to think about what he had seen.

Berridin waited until Ellara and Meri finished speaking, then approached Meri himself.

**Berridin:** "Meri? I was just wondering something, you know, when I first met Ysidor, I thought all stone Goliaths acted like that, and then I met you, and so I'm just wondering if there's something special about Ysidor, or do we have to treat him a certain way?"

Meri still thought Berridin was a child.

**Meri:** "Ysidor is not a complicated individual. His heart is in the right place, and probably his most admirable feature. After all, I did choose him to be my successor."

She described him as direct rather than complicated, young and inexperienced, perhaps a little coddled by the village, but fundamentally admirable.

**Berridin:** "Yeah, Ysidor pretty cool."

Berridin said he liked Ysidor and was glad Meri had chosen him. Then he asked about the selection process: who had been runner-up, or what the backup plan was.

**Meri:** "Strange question from a friend."

In truth, she said, Hýr guides them. There was no runner-up. It was what it was. When Berridin pressed the hypothetical, Meri did not soften it.

**Meri:** "No, there is no if. Should anything happen to my Little Hill, there is no one now to succeed. I'm waiting. He is my successor."

Ahead of them, Ysidor tripped and fell.

**Ysidor:** "Oh, fuck."

**Ysidor:** "God works in mysterious ways."

Meri pointed out that there were no recent records she knew of where anyone had heard the voice of the Goddess so clearly. Yet it had happened during Ysidor’s time.

**Meri:** "Maybe that's a sign."

Berridin admitted that despite Ysidor’s innocence, Ysidor had probably already saved his life. At times, he trusted him.

**Meri:** "You could trust Ysidor as someone that you should trust without fail. I do not think he would ever fail you."

She thought Ysidor would probably die trying not to fail them.

Berridin apologized and walked awkwardly beside her in silence until she turned toward her house. He tried to explain the question as curiosity about the village’s geopolitical process.

**Meri:** "I think sometimes perspectives in a city and in a quiet village like this, some ideas do not apply."

Before leaving, she thanked him for what he and the others had done for the village.

**Meri:** "You... Little Hill and all the friends he's made truly appreciate it."

Berridin apologized for getting caught, calling it a slight hiccup.

**Meri:** "I'm used to slight hiccups."

Then she went home to sleep.

---

## 7) Nighty-Nighty — The Long Rest

Witty was beyond exhausted, nearly a walking corpse, and wanted only to sleep. He sat outside and started nodding off against a wall.

Berridin, Ellara, and Ysidor were the ones close enough to notice if something was off (**Berridin, Ellara, and Ysidor Perception; need 10+**). Ellara noticed Witty settling outside.

He had tucked himself into an alcove beside a building, feet up, shoes off and neatly set aside. He pulled out a long sleeping cap, put it on, and tugged it down over his eyes. He had a little stuffed animal. All in purple, very city-looking and wildly out of place, he curled in his cloak with his feet out.

**Witty:** "Nighty-nighty."

The mountain night was chilly, maybe 12–15 C.

**Witty:** "The Whitaker keep their babies outside to sleep in the cold to increase our strength and fertility."

**Witty:** "It didn't work."

Ellara found a blanket, likely from Ysidor’s place, and brought it out to cover him. Witty grunted and rolled over.

**Witty:** "Thanks, mom, mama."

**Ellara:** "Mom, not like Jeeves or something? It's been a long day."

Nyx remained outside against Ysidor’s house. Berridin, Ysidor, and Ellara went indoors.

Long rest.
