# Session 21 — *The Lives Within the Light*

**Locales:** Whitman Manor (Bastion City) · Seminary grounds → Proctor Eduard’s office → Ellara’s room

**Time:** Two days after the return to Bastion; late afternoon → evening → night → following morning

---

## 1) After the Mountain — A House Saved, a Purpose Uncertain

The army lay buried in a cave-in near **Hýrda**. The party had rushed back to Bastion to meet the bank’s deadline, where **Berridin—Din—** persuaded the bank and paid the administrative fees that saved **Witty’s house from foreclosure**. Two days later, everyone reunited at Whitman Manor. Berridin had stayed at Nyx’s in the meantime and was fully recovered from his exhaustion.

With the immediate emergencies behind them, Nyx asked what came next. Witty raised the possibility of applying for **Michelle Faberge’s vacant position** at the Duskbreaker Lighthouse. Nyx saw that as a way inside, but wanted a purpose before a tactic.

Ysidor reported that his village was safe **for now**, with Meri handling matters there. He did not expect the people trapped in the mountain to emerge anytime soon. The weight of what they had done was only beginning to settle.

**Ysidor:** “We did a really fucked up thing. Just processed that now.”

**Berridin:** “I think it’s a war crime.”

**Nyx:** “It’s not a war crime, it’s a war suggestion.”

Beneath the joke, Nyx had no clear next step. He thought in contracts: finish the job, get paid, move on. Keeping Meri from being harvested to power machines had given him a concrete objective. Now he looked to the others for what should replace it.

Their finances offered little reassurance. Witty insisted they would be fine, then wandered through financial terminology he did not seem to understand. He claimed they had at least **26 gold**; Berridin said he had none and declared them financially devastated. Disguising himself as an accountant, complete with a little hat and calculator, he called for immediate donations to the foundation.

Nyx was reduced to hoping Berridin had stolen something useful.

Berridin assured him that, out of respect, he had left Nyx’s belongings alone—**apart from the safe**. Nyx thanked him, registered that last detail, and left it for another day.

## 2) Trust and Consequences — Whether to Tell Eduard

Ellara had not fully absorbed what had happened. Her instinct was to discuss it with **Proctor Eduard**; surely he would know what to do.

Nyx was blunt: he did not trust the Proctor. Ellara objected to his claim that the outcome was “50-50,” pointing out that uncertainty did not make two possibilities equally likely. Nyx’s concern was practical rather than mathematical: if Eduard was involved, telling him what they knew could end in imprisonment or disappearance.

Ysidor gave the warning a more personal foundation. Meri had told him Eduard **might have been involved in her abduction and torture**. Ellara called that hearsay; Ysidor rejected the description.

For Ellara, refusing to speak carried its own moral risk.

**Ellara:** “I don’t know that I could live with myself if I didn’t do everything possible to get to the truth.”

Nyx recognized the motive, but urged her to consider an indirect approach. People hiding the truth could be warned by the very questions meant to expose them. Ellara asked him, again, not to patronize her.

He finally put the fear plainly: he had seen people disappear or die because dangerous information reached the wrong ears. He did not want anyone in the room killed because they had trusted the wrong person.

**Ellara:** “So you can take the risk of people dying if I don’t talk to him?”

Nyx had no satisfying answer. Neither did Ellara when Witty asked what happened if they died because she *did* speak.

As afternoon became evening, the argument exhausted itself. Nyx could see the shortcomings of the whole group, himself included; these were still the people they had to work with. Ellara promised to **think about speaking to Eduard**, leaving the decision unresolved.

Witty’s request for a recommendation from the Proctor earned an initial “no,” followed by Ellara’s more diplomatic concession: “If the light wills it.”

---

## 3) A Hundred Lamps — The Crystal’s Hidden Matrix

Berridin turned their attention to the **large light crystal** recovered on their journey. Ysidor took it from his bag and tossed it over. Roughly head-sized, it was lighter than expected.

They set it on the table. At first it resembled an enlarged ordinary crystal, but Berridin suggested Witty conduct the examination—a useful test for someone considering Faberge’s position. Ysidor placed a hand on Witty’s shoulder and bestowed **Hýr’s blessing**, a yellowish glow granting **advantage on Intelligence checks**.

With **Witty’s Investigation 19**, the differences became clear. Ordinary light crystals had a light-emitting core; this one contained a much more intricate internal matrix. Most of its weight sat in a dense, solid core beneath a lighter outer shell. It retained some charge, but its altered composition would not illuminate like an ordinary lamp. Witty suspected a steady **bluish glow**, almost like a salt lamp.

Without laboratory testing, he estimated its power output at **about one hundred times that of a normal light crystal**.

Berridin asked whether it could power a bomb. Witty answered that any power source could become an explosive with the right knowledge—and this one could produce a very large reaction.

Light crystals were **extremely stable**. Compromising this crystal’s stability would nevertheless cause catastrophic destruction. Witty thought feeding more energy into the matrix than it could hold might overload it, but he did not know how much that would require. No one had seen a light crystal explode before; the kinetic force would be considerable, while any secondary effects remained unknown.

**Nyx:** “Are we terrorists now?”

The more consequential discovery lay in the matrix itself. Light crystals could form naturally from decaying living matter; deposits near the ancient ruins explained the interest in the mines, providing an easier source than converting people. This crystal’s structure, however, was **too uniform to be natural**. Witty’s examination identified it as **artificial**.

Ysidor asked how many people might have died to make something this size. Witty could offer only “a lot.” They had no count, but the possibility settled heavily over the table.

**Nyx:** “If it’s Shadowed being used to create these crystals, that’s me. That’s most of us here. We’re being used as fuel.”

Ellara called it an abomination and insisted they stop the creation of more. Witty struggled to reconcile it with scientific principles—or with what he had believed Michelle Faberge stood for.

For the first time that afternoon, they had a shared objective.

Berridin returned to the employment plan: Witty’s family name might get someone inside the Lighthouse, where they could learn what it was doing. Nyx agreed.

**Nyx:** “Can’t let her death be in vain. I agree, Din. We gotta do something. I have no plan. That’s the best damn thing I’ve heard all day.”

## 4) Whose Last Rites? — Two Blessings Around One Crystal

Before bed, Berridin asked Ellara to bless the lives lost in the crystal’s making. He gathered everyone around it and took Nyx’s hand.

Nyx found the arrangement uncomfortably cult-like, especially when they were circling the very object they wanted to destroy. Tired enough to let it happen, he agreed if it would get them to bed sooner.

Ellara doubted Berridin’s intentions, but accepted the purpose: the souls involved deserved repose. She began a religious ritual, **without casting a specific spell**, making an **Ellara Religion 14** check.

Ysidor interrupted. If these were Shadowed lives, he did not believe their last rites should belong to the faith associated with their suffering. He wanted to offer **Hýr’s blessing** instead.

**Ellara:** “The light is for everybody.”

**Ysidor:** “Maybe, but they didn’t feel welcome when they were alive. I don’t think they’ll feel very welcome when they’re dead.”

Ellara insisted the atrocity was not of the Light. Blaming the Light for it made no more sense to her than blaming Hýr whenever a relationship failed. Ysidor answered that the Light could not be forced on those who had not chosen it, then declared he would not allow the blessing.

Still holding hands, he took Ellara’s hand, touched the crystal, and began a deep, stone-reverberating throat song, asking that the dead return to Hýr’s arms. He cast **Ceremony**, holding the circle together for an hour.

Ellara quietly continued her own blessing. Her **Deception 17** went unnoticed against **Ysidor’s passive Perception 10**. He wanted to believe she was a good person and did not actively scrutinize her.

The crystal received both rites. The disagreement remained.

By nightfall, the west-side street light crystals were out. Nyx curled up in a corner, pulled on his hood, and entered his trance. Witty had somehow changed into purple silk pajamas and a nightcap, with a teddy bear under his arm; at one point, he substituted the bear for himself in the circle.

Berridin hurried to claim the master bed, arguing that he had paid for the house. Ysidor stayed as well, refusing to roam the streets. Ellara chose to return to her nearby room at the Seminary.

## 5) A Fatherly Hand — Ellara Tells the Proctor

On the Seminary grounds, Ellara crossed paths with **Eduard**. He welcomed her back from her journey. When she began to tear up, he placed a hand on her shoulder and offered either a conversation now or an open door when she was rested.

**Ellara’s Insight 26** found **sincere, fatherly concern** in the offer.

She accepted. The path of the Light, usually so clear to her, had become cloudy.

**Eduard:** “While the light can shine, it can be obscured. It does not always clear the path we walk. Sometimes we walk in faith.”

In his office, over tea, Ellara struggled to explain her fear that the Duke was using the Light to build his own power while working against it. Eduard acknowledged his associations with the Duke and his role as the Eulogia’s contact with the Lighthouse. He began to describe research that could seem violent.

Ellara stopped him.

**Ellara:** “It doesn’t seem violent. They are killing people to make these crystals. It’s not right. There’s no way the light would allow such a thing.”

Eduard was taken aback. She explained that living Shadowed were being used: the stone goliath she had previously asked about had escaped, an army had subsequently gone to her village, and a huge crystal had been made. Ellara did not know how many lives it had cost.

Eduard said his understanding, based on the Lighthouse’s account, was that **light crystals were mined from ancient ruins**. He had understood the army’s northern deployment as a matter of territorial disputes. Hostage-taking and experiments on living people were a far graver claim.

His earlier inquiries had gone slowly. The Eulogia’s position with the Lighthouse was weak, and **Donnathan Reeves had been slippery**, repeatedly putting him off. Reeves’s closeness to the Duke made that route no more promising.

Eduard also reported news that the **head researcher had died, with foul play suspected**. He wondered whether she had known too much. Ellara’s eyes flickered down before she looked back at him.

Ellara maintained that she had witnessed the abuse herself. Whatever those responsible claimed, **Nites would not suffer it**. Eduard agreed.

She warned him to be careful; she did not want him disappearing because of what she had told him. Eduard considered a move against him much more difficult, but feared for **her** if her knowledge became known.

He proposed a way out: perhaps it was time for her apprenticeship to continue at the heart of the church in **Pharus**. The church had no authority over the Duke, but she should be safer within its walls in the capital. There would also be a much larger library for her research.

Ellara could not decide whether to leave, go home, or remain. Eduard offered to prepare **writs for rail passage**, with the Seminary paying her transit, and asked her to return after resting.

Before leaving, she spoke quietly.

**Ellara:** “I knew it. I knew I could trust you.”

Eduard answered her uncertainty with an admission that even he knew dark nights of the soul. When the Light could not be seen, they could still walk in faith.

**Eduard:** “Dawn will always come again, child. Even with clouds, when you cannot see the rising sun, dawn shall come.”

As she left, he began writing.

## 6) An Unsettled Spirit — A Ticket Without a Date

In her room, Ellara meditated with her **highland herbs** and consulted her spirit, hoping to quiet her heart.

On **Ellara’s d100 roll of 86**, the spirit offered no further clue. It felt unnerved, uncertain, shaken, and sad—almost a reflection of her own feelings.

She comforted it mentally, as though cradling a cat.

**Ellara:** “Yes, I understand. I feel it too. At least we have each other.”

The following morning, a plain envelope waited under her door, her name written meticulously on the front. Inside was a **writ of passage signed by Proctor Eduard**, authorizing rail travel to Pharus at the church’s expense. It carried **no date**, functioning as a ticket she could use when needed.

His accompanying note read: “I hope you reconsider my offer.”

After her morning prayers, Ellara considered the offer and her companions. For now, she chose to rejoin them.

## 7) The Valet and the Allowance — Witty Finds Another Account

At Whitman Manor, Witty and Berridin woke in the master bed together, Witty the little spoon. Berridin had been hired as his valet, but their first morning exposed a disagreement about what employment actually required.

Witty expected shower supplies and the comforts of home. Berridin wanted an advance on his salary so he could afford to provide them. Each insisted the other needed to act first.

The argument moved to Witty’s missing clothes and money. Berridin had Witty’s wallet, but Witty explained that a gentleman was never far from his purse: he slept with his remaining money in a pouch around his neck.

Finding no gold in the discarded underwear, Berridin threw it at Witty. **Berridin’s Unarmed Strike Nat 1** left it unfolding harmlessly onto the floor between them. He stomped out.

By the front door, beside Witty’s actual trousers, Berridin found and opened an envelope addressed to **Whitaker Whitman VI**. **Countess Tallulah Whitman** wrote that his parents were leaving their southern estate for **Pharus**, where business would keep them for the next few months. If he needed anything, he should write to their address there.

Berridin brought writing supplies and the opened letter back upstairs, pressing Witty to request money.

That conversation uncovered a more immediate resource. After Witty’s last request, his parents had put him on an **allowance**. It had been sufficient to pay **Jarvis**, though Jarvis had robbed him blind. Crucially, the deposits had continued while Witty was away, into **a different account from the one Berridin had dealt with at the bank**.

Witty could withdraw money. He also proposed making Berridin his **signatory**, allowing him to manage these affairs and continue their employment arrangement. Berridin readily agreed; the banking changes and withdrawal still lay ahead.

Elsewhere, Nyx found coffee and made it. Ysidor was still sleeping when Ellara arrived later that morning.

---

## 8) Coffee and Admissions — Nyx and Ellara Find Common Ground

Nyx cautiously checked the door, recognized Ellara through the window, and let her in. He offered coffee; she accepted.

Witty and Berridin could be heard arguing elsewhere in the house. Nyx dismissed it as some spat about money and service. The abandoned trousers by the entrance seemed to fit.

Ellara hesitated over how to describe her night. She had found some peace of mind, but wondered whether the danger had grown beyond them. Perhaps they should let the **Eulogia** handle it.

Nyx answered almost in a whisper. He shared the fear that they could not accomplish what needed doing. He also softened his warning from the previous evening: any of them could say the wrong thing and get someone killed.

Ellara suggested Pharus, where they might be protected at the heart of the church. Nyx drew a distinction: **she** might find protection there; he did not expect the same for himself, Ysidor, or most of the others. He could not protect her as much as he wanted to, and leaving remained a real choice.

Yet walking away offered no peace either.

**Nyx:** “But we’re going to see another fucking crystal. This wasn’t the first. You and I both know that.”

Then, with difficulty, he reconsidered his own position. With so few leads, perhaps speaking to Eduard was a good idea.

Ellara told him she already had.

Nyx’s eyes widened. First he checked that she was safe; then he asked what she had learned. Ellara described Eduard’s apparent shock, his support, and his wish to get her somewhere safer. To her, his response showed that the Eulogia was not involved and that she had been right to trust him.

Nyx remained uncertain about the Proctor’s role, but understood why the conversation had felt natural to her. Eduard was family in all but name.

**Nyx:** “I’ve lived long enough to see just about everyone betray me at some point. That’s where I’m coming from.”

Ellara was still not ready to abandon the work. She wanted to know what else she could do to stop more crystals being made. Nyx accepted that the choice was hers and, in an unusually open moment, told her he had always seen her as the group’s level-headed member.

They still needed information, especially about the Duke’s knowledge.

The political picture mattered: **the Lighthouse was independent**. Michelle Faberge had run its research division, while Reeves handled public relations. The Duke supported it financially for Bastion and Hieroterra’s interests, gaining money and prestige, but **did not run it**. Reeves was his childhood best friend and longtime lover, making their connection both political and personal.

None of that confirmed **what the Duke knew about the experiments**. Ellara pointed to his deployment of the army and his power to intervene, but neither she nor Nyx could establish how far his knowledge extended.

Returning to the Lighthouse by their previous methods was hardly promising. They needed another way inside.

---

## 9) Change It from Within — Witty’s Application

Nyx suggested sending Witty.

Witty entered with a bowl of cereal and no trousers. Once that fact had been acknowledged, he made his case: the Lighthouse was an institution he had admired all his life, and he could not accept what it had become.

**Witty:** “I need to make it right and change it from the inside.”

His initial strategy was charm. His more tangible advantage was family: **Donnathan Reeves knew the Whitmans**, who came from the same region, potentially giving Witty political access.

**Witty:** “Nepotism. This is the key. Nepotism.”

He envisioned a nice suit, direct eye contact, and a firm handshake—the method his father had taught him for getting a job.

The party settled on the plan: **Witty would apply at the Duskbreaker Lighthouse**, using his credentials and family connections to seek access to the institution they needed to understand.

The application itself was still ahead.
